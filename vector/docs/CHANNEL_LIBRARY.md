# Channel Library

This is a practical library of common channels VECTOR may evaluate.

## 1) Direct DM / email

Best when:
- ICP is identifiable
- trigger moment is obvious
- the founder can personalize quickly

Strengths:
- fast signal
- easy to measure
- low setup cost

Weaknesses:
- can feel spammy
- response quality varies
- poor targeting is immediately visible

## 2) Community participation

Best when:
- ICP gathers in niche communities
- credibility matters more than raw volume
- the founder can contribute value before selling

Strengths:
- relationship building
- better trust
- durable network effects

Weaknesses:
- slow warm-up
- hidden attribution
- requires patience

## 3) X / Twitter

Best when:
- the ICP already follows niche accounts
- the product can be framed in a strong insight
- the founder can post consistently

Strengths:
- fast reach among builders
- content can seed DMs and conversations
- good for audience-driven products

Weaknesses:
- noisy
- easy to mistake likes for demand
- signal quality depends on ICP presence

## 4) LinkedIn

Best when:
- the ICP is professional and role-based
- the offer is B2B or work-related
- relationship and credibility matter

Strengths:
- clear professional identity
- useful for outbound and warm intros
- good for role-specific pain

Weaknesses:
- many users are ignoring generic outreach
- needs personalization
- can be slow if the angle is weak

## 5) Marketplaces / directories

Best when:
- the venue already has buying intent
- the category is well understood
- trust can be inherited from the platform

Strengths:
- intent-rich traffic
- easier discovery
- vendor trust can be borrowed

Weaknesses:
- competition can be intense
- fees / platform dependence
- often a venue problem, not a demand problem

## 6) Content / SEO

Best when:
- the problem is searchable
- the product has long-term compounding value
- the team can sustain content output

Strengths:
- compounding traffic
- educational authority
- strong long-term asset

Weaknesses:
- slow feedback loop
- poor fit for immediate 0→1 unless the founder already has distribution leverage

## 7) Partnerships

Best when:
- the product complements another workflow
- another business already has the audience
- the partner can credibly refer users

Strengths:
- trust transfer
- leverage
- potentially high-quality leads

Weaknesses:
- negotiation friction
- dependency risk
- slower setup

## v1.6 channel archetype expansion

### Channel archetypes
- **Direct response**: cold email, cold DM, warm DM, outbound community outreach.
- **Conversation channels**: LinkedIn, Discord, Slack, niche forums, private groups.
- **Content channels**: X threads, short-form posts, long-form articles, tutorials.
- **Owned channels**: email list, newsletter, community, in-product prompts.
- **Marketplace channels**: app stores, templates, directories, product marketplaces.
- **Partner channels**: agencies, consultants, adjacent creators, integrations, referrals.

### Use the archetype before the tactic
Do not ask “Should we do X?”
Ask:
- Which archetype fits the ICP?
- How fast can it create signal?
- How much trust is needed?
- Can the founder sustain the volume?

### When to favor each archetype
- **Direct response** when the ICP is known and the founder can personalize.
- **Conversation channels** when the ICP is already active in communities.
- **Content channels** when the founder has a credible point of view and patience.
- **Owned channels** when retention and compounding matter.
- **Marketplace channels** when discovery is intent-driven.
- **Partner channels** when trust transfer is the bottleneck.
